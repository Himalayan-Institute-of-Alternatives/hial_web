ABOUT THE FELLOWSHIP:

The Himalayan region is experiencing rapid environmental change, with implications for water availability, mountain ecosystems, agriculture, and the livelihoods of local communities. In high-altitude cold deserts such as Ladakh, where precipitation is limited and water availability is closely linked to seasonal snow and glacier melt, developing locally appropriate water-management solutions is increasingly important.

The Ice Stupa Fellowship at the Himalayan Institute of Alternatives, Ladakh (HIAL) aims to bring together students and emerging researchers from engineering, physical sciences, environmental sciences, architecture, and related disciplines to explore innovative technological solutions for water-related challenges in the Himalayas.

The fellowship will use Ice Stupa technology as a starting point for interdisciplinary research, engineering, experimentation, and innovation. Participants will engage with real-world problems through field-oriented research, prototyping, modelling, and testing, while exploring how scientific knowledge and engineering design can be adapted to challenging mountain environments.

Key details of the Fellowship:

  • Duration: 6 months, fully residential (winter months, November to April)
  • Location: HIAL campus, Phyang, Leh-UT Ladakh, with field stays in villages across Ladakh
  • Eligibility: Applicants from the Himalayan region of India, Nepal and Bhutan
  • Fee: No course fee — participants cover food and accommodation at Rs 10,000 per month

WHY ICE STUPAS?

Ice Stupas are artificial ice structures developed to store water in frozen form during winter and release it as they melt in spring. In suitable locations, this approach can help make water available during periods when agricultural demand begins to rise but natural water sources may be limited.

The technology also raises important research and engineering questions. How can water be transported and frozen efficiently under varying climatic conditions? How can artificial ice systems be designed for different terrain and water requirements? What roles can sensors, automation, predictive modelling, and new materials play in improving their performance?

These questions extend beyond Ice Stupas themselves. They connect to broader challenges in mountain hydrology, cryosphere research, environmental monitoring, structural engineering, and climate-responsive infrastructure.

The fellowship seeks to create a learning and research environment in which participants can investigate these questions and develop solutions grounded in scientific understanding and local realities.

OBJECTIVES OF THE FELLOWSHIP:

The fellowship aims to:

  • Build interdisciplinary understanding of Ice Stupa technology, artificial ice systems, and mountain water challenges.
  • Encourage field-oriented research into the engineering and environmental problems associated with high-altitude water systems.
  • Support the development, prototyping, modelling, and testing of innovative technological solutions.
  • Explore the application of automation, Internet of Things (IoT), sensing systems, and autonomous technologies in mountain environments.
  • Connect engineering and scientific research with the practical needs of Himalayan communities.
  • Foster collaboration among students, scientists, researchers, engineers, and scholars from diverse disciplines.

RESEARCH AND LEARNING THEMES:

The fellowship will explore the following themes:

  • Ice Stupa and Artificial Ice Systems
  • Mountain Hydrology
  • Automation and Autonomous Technologies
  • IoT, Sensing and Wireless Systems
  • Mechanical and Materials Engineering
  • Structural Design and Architecture
  • Modelling, Simulation and Algorithms
  • Glaciology and Cryosphere Research

WHAT WILL FELLOWS DO?

During the fellowship, participants will engage with practical research and engineering challenges through activities such as:

  • Field-oriented research: Examine real-world problems related to artificial ice systems, water availability, and high-altitude environments.
  • Design and innovation: Develop ideas and engineering approaches to address identified challenges.
  • Prototyping and experimentation: Build, test, and improve prototypes, components, or technological solutions.
  • Modelling and simulation: Apply analytical and computational methods to understand system performance and evaluate design alternatives.
  • Interdisciplinary collaboration: Work with scientists, researchers, engineers, and fellow participants from different academic backgrounds.
  • Field studies and observation: Investigate the environmental and operational conditions that influence the performance of technologies in Himalayan settings.

WHO CAN APPLY?

People only from Himalayan region in India and Nepal & Bhutan, aged 23 to 30, with relevant academic backgrounds are eligible to apply, including those studying:

  • Civil Engineering and Architecture
  • Mechanical Engineering and Materials Science
  • Electronics and Electrical Engineering
  • Computer Science, IoT, and Automation
  • Physics
  • Glaciology and Hydrology
  • Environmental Science and Climate Science

We are also open to those people beyond the above background to apply but having deep interest and keen in working with Artificial Glaciers systems in their own regions to help community facing water scarcity.

WHY JOIN THE FELLOWSHIP?

The Ice Stupa Fellowship offers an opportunity to connect academic knowledge with real-world technological challenges in a distinctive Himalayan environment.

Fellows will have the opportunity to:

  • Explore an emerging area of climate-responsive water technology.
  • Apply their academic knowledge to practical research and engineering problems.
  • Gain experience in experimentation, prototyping, modelling, and field-based investigation.
  • Learn from and collaborate with people from different scientific and technical disciplines.
  • Explore how technologies can be designed for extreme temperatures, high altitudes, and demanding terrain.
  • Contribute ideas towards improving the understanding, design, monitoring, and performance of artificial ice systems.

OUR APPROACH:

At HIAL, the fellowship will emphasise learning through engagement with real problems. Participants will be encouraged to question existing approaches, examine evidence, test ideas, learn from past experiments, and refine their solutions.

The programme brings together scientific inquiry, engineering practice, environmental understanding, and interdisciplinary collaboration. Its central premise is that meaningful technological innovation emerges when rigorous research is connected to the conditions in which a technology must actually function.

THE LARGER VISION:

The Ice Stupa Fellowship seeks to nurture a community of emerging researchers and innovators who can contribute to the development of appropriate technologies for the Himalayan region and other challenging environments.

By connecting Ice Stupa technology with wider questions in hydrology, cryosphere science, engineering, sensing, automation, and design, the fellowship aims to encourage solutions that are scientifically informed, context-sensitive, and responsive to the realities of mountain environments.

Join us in exploring how science, engineering, and interdisciplinary collaboration can contribute to more resilient water systems in the Himalayas.
